# Cold editorial read and manuscript voice

Use after the science is checked. House style is subordinate to accuracy, necessary uncertainty, article type, and current venue requirements. [Scientific editing](scientific-humanization.md) governs the meaning ledger and revision permissions.

## Read as the intended reader

Read uninterrupted, then reverse-outline substantial drafts as one sentence per paragraph. Can a reader recover the question, design, population/comparison, primary findings, uncertainty, and justified conclusion without the repository or chat? Does each paragraph have a scientific job? Repair missing logic before changing individual words.

Lead with the point the reader needs, then its support. Introduction establishes the gap before the objective. Methods follows reproducible procedure. Results commonly begins with participant flow/cohort before primary endpoints. Abstract structure follows article type and venue. Do not force every section to announce its conclusion first.

State estimates plainly; calibrate interpretation to precision, design, missingness, multiplicity, and transportability. Avoid hedge piles, but retain qualifiers that change what is known. Confidence does not depend on crossing a P-value threshold.

## Scientific transparency belongs in the paper

Place detailed bias discussion in limitations while retaining design boundaries near claims and in the abstract when needed. Describe rationale for analytic choices when readers need it to assess validity or reproduce methods. Report protocol amendments, excluded analyses, missingness, deviations, and post hoc decisions where reporting guidance or scientific transparency requires them. Do not hide these as 'project history'.

Reproducible software versions, repository/data accession links, registration identifiers, and required AI disclosure are valid methods or administrative content. Internal task names, drafting deliberation, file-management explanations, and unresolved author notes usually are not. Project vocabulary ("frozen", "locked", "final model", "canonical", "v2", "the corrected cohort") describes the team's journey and stays out of every deliverable, including supplements, captions, cover letters and code comments that will be shared. Reviewer responses appropriately say what was revised and why.

## Preserve a defensible voice

Prefer exact conventional biomedical terms, clear subjects, direct verbs, stable terminology, and given-to-new flow. Passive voice, first person, nominal terms, repeated technical words, and punctuation may be appropriate. Match supplied author samples through traits without copying phrases or importing facts.

Question vague importance claims, formulaic transitions, generic endings, synonym churn, and ornamental language only when they weaken meaning or readability. Literal terms such as robust standard errors, pivotal trial, or mutational landscape are not defects. No lexical cue proves AI authorship; do not optimize detector outcomes.

## Match the passage's job

A Discussion opening establishes the main contribution and develops its meaning. Do not reflexively turn its last paragraph into a limitations inventory or a call for prospective validation. In a full Discussion those topics have later homes; a requested opening is not a miniature complete paper. Include a constraint here only when this immediate interpretation would otherwise be inaccurate.

Similarly, do not insert every secondary covariate or subgroup into the opening simply to demonstrate balance. Include it only if it advances or changes the central interpretation. Avoid repeating detailed Results estimates unless the comparison itself drives the argument. Do not drift from an association paper into an unrequested prediction-development agenda or a generic clinical recommendation.

This is direct scientific interpretation, not maximal assertion. Prefer a supported positive statement about what the study establishes over a sequence of “could,” “compatible with,” “does not establish,” and “worth testing.” One precise inferential qualifier is enough when needed. Do not append a generic residual-confounding disclaimer to a calibrated association simply to demonstrate caution; consolidate that design limitation later unless the local sentence would otherwise overclaim. An absence of measured mechanism need not become a repeated disclaimer if the text makes no mechanistic claim.

## Sentence economy and nonformulaic flow

Read the paper as an argument rather than a collection of polished sentences. Name the central scientific contribution and each paragraph's contribution to it. If two paragraphs do the same job, combine or remove redundant material. If the next idea feels unmotivated, add the missing scientific link or reorder; an ornamental transition cannot fix it.

For each sentence ask what new observation, explanation, distinction or implication it contributes. If deleting it leaves the scientific reasoning intact, delete or merge it before delivery. Do not leave a sentence you already recognize as filler and merely explain the weakness in editorial notes. Replace announcements such as “this is scientifically interesting” with the actual scientific distinction, or omit them. Prefer direct explanation to rhetorical questions. Delete sentences that only announce importance, restate the paragraph, explain document construction, or hedge an already calibrated statement. Vary structure as reasoning demands, without decorative synonym changes, contrived triads, repeated concession pivots or a mandatory closing summary in every paragraph.

Do not allocate discussion length by the number of available analyses. Focus on the central contribution, develop supporting evidence, and report less consequential findings briefly. A finding that undermines the central claim deserves a response; a peripheral result does not deserve equal airtime simply because it is less supportive. See [narrative framing](narrative-frameworks.md).

## Compact captions

Write the title/caption a reader needs to identify and interpret the display. Give the comparison, population/timepoint or model only when unclear, and define unfamiliar symbols/intervals/abbreviations. Omit descriptions of obvious colors, axes, left/right placement and chart construction. Do not duplicate the Results, statistical Methods, or limitations. Put necessary technical definitions in brief footnotes; do not bury clinically important qualifications where interpretation would be misleading.

## Final questions

- Does the reader see primary outcomes, harms, and material contrary findings in proportion to their scientific role?
- Are every comparison and causal/modal statement supported? Is any caveat so important that it should narrow the neighboring claim?
- Are titles/labels informative, captions self-contained, and units/uncertainty/denominators clear?
- Are citations close to the supported claims, with enough sources for the evidence rather than an arbitrary quota?
- Did edits preserve signs, values, citation scope, endpoint roles, and prespecification?
- Is any publication content actually internal management language, or any necessary reporting detail mistakenly removed as such?
- If the text was revised after the analysis or project changed, does any phrase lean on a version the reader never saw ("the corrected cohort", "now", "no longer")? See the newcomer read in [prose register](prose-register.md).
- Did the register pass add anything: a number, citation, stake, hedge or em dash the source did not have?

Mechanical lint is advisory for style. Retain sound wording and document material adjudications for package work. Never rewrite correct scientific prose merely to obtain zero warnings.
