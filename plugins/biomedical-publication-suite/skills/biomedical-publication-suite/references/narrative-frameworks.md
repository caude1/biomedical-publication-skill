# Narrative and grant-adjacent frameworks

Use for scientific story reconstruction, a requested narrative audit, stronger argument, or proposal narrative. This does not expand authority to invent facts or turn exploratory observations into prespecified hypotheses.

## Discover the organizing contribution

Read the study question, final results, displays and relevant literature before deciding what the paper is about. Consider the strongest plausible frames: an unexpected pattern, a clinically consequential difference, a resolution of conflicting evidence, a useful negative finding, a methodological advance, or a mechanistic connection. Compare them privately by scientific importance, explanatory power, surprise/novelty supported by actual literature, evidentiary strength and relevance to the intended reader. Do not equate novelty with an unverified “first,” importance with a small P value, or narrative strength with favorable results.

Choose one principal contribution and express its argument in a short chain: what is known → the unresolved tension → why this study addresses it → the pivotal finding → what that finding changes. Let that chain determine the abstract emphasis, paragraph order, main displays and Discussion. Supporting findings should explain, strengthen, qualify or bound that contribution. A long inventory of outputs is not a story.

Let the requested passage do its own job. In a Discussion opening, develop the central contribution before moving to later limitations or research proposals. Do not add an incremental-prediction claim or validation agenda merely because the study reports an adjusted association.

The central insight can be unexpected or exploratory when that is its actual status and the paper reports it honestly. Do not invent a prospective hypothesis or hide the original primary endpoint to make the frame work. A useful null primary result may itself motivate an explanatory secondary finding. Distinguish interesting observation from confirmed mechanism.

## Allocate attention deliberately

Rank findings by their bearing on the central scientific question, evidentiary strength, novelty/importance and clinical or safety consequence. Prespecification and endpoint role constrain interpretation and required reporting; they do not require equal prose for every analysis.

- **Central:** develop the finding and its scientific meaning, supported by the key evidence.
- **Supporting:** include the detail needed to make the central reasoning convincing.
- **Boundary-changing:** explain contradictory evidence or harms enough to calibrate the central claim; revise that claim if needed.
- **Secondary/contextual:** report accurately and briefly, often in one sentence, table or supplement with an appropriate pointer. Do not give a weakly related null or less supportive result a full defensive paragraph merely because it exists.
- **Internal/irrelevant:** omit discarded exploratory branches, implementation details and draft decisions unless an actual methods/transparency requirement makes them material.

Proportionate emphasis is good editing. Do not apologize for every secondary result, recite every sensitivity analysis, or repeatedly revisit a limitation already stated. If contrary evidence does not alter the main inference, acknowledge it concisely and move on. If it does alter the inference, recalibrate the story rather than drowning the reader in hedges.

## Build cumulative reasoning

Each paragraph has one scientific job and adds something the preceding paragraph made the reader ready to understand. Connect ideas through their actual relationship, not repeated “moreover” or “importantly.” A reader should be able to explain why paragraph B follows paragraph A.

Within paragraphs, move from a precise point to the evidence or reasoning needed for that point, then advance the argument. Do not force a topic/evidence/restate pattern on every paragraph. Delete ending sentences that merely repeat the opening, background already established, Results re-listed in Discussion without interpretation, generic praise and boilerplate future-work claims. Stable technical terms should repeat when necessary; ideas should not repeat without a new purpose.

Keep an objective when no hypothesis existed. Organize exposition coherently without recounting research chronology. Preserve essential protocol/analysis disclosures and the distinction between observed findings and proposed mechanisms.

For a requested narrative audit, return external-facing **Scientific Narrative** followed by internal **Editorial Rationale** unless another format is requested. Rationale records structural decisions, evidence gaps/conflicts, material claim recalibration, and why results were featured or subordinated. It is not part of manuscript prose.

## Stronger language

Find the clearest supported answer; remove empty throat-clearing and unsupported importance adjectives. Report estimates directly without erasing imprecision. Explain clinical or mechanistic meaning only through a defensible evidence bridge. Explicitly hypothetical mechanisms remain hypothetical.

Name specific limitations and the inference they threaten. State bias direction only when justified; 'direction uncertain' may be correct. Keep limitations near claims when necessary, including the abstract. A limitations section cannot repair an overstated conclusion.

## Grant or project narrative

Separate preliminary findings from proposed work, expected results from observed results, and feasibility evidence from aspiration. Each aim needs question, approach, dependencies, feasibility support, and supported contingency options. Do not invent power, access to patients/data/equipment, collaborators, approvals, pilot results, institutional commitments, or funder requirements. Use `grant_feasibility_ledger.csv` only when aim-level tracking helps (see [schemas](schemas.md)).

Ambitious proposals may describe a plausible future test; label it as a plan and disclose unresolved dependencies outside clean scientific text. Current funder instructions govern structure, page limits, AI disclosure, and required attachments.
